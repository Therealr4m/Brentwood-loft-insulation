How Thick Should Rigid PIR Loft Insulation Be?

Across Brentwood we regularly see lofts where traditional mineral wool isn't always the best solution. Some loft conversions, storage areas and restricted roof spaces simply don't have enough room for 270mm of loft roll insulation. This is where rigid PIR insulation boards often become a practical option.

While PIR insulation delivers excellent thermal performance for its thickness, choosing the correct board depth is important. Too thin and the insulation may not achieve the desired performance. Too thick and it can create installation difficulties or unnecessary cost.

What Is Rigid PIR Insulation?

PIR stands for Polyisocyanurate. It is a rigid foam insulation board commonly used in lofts, roofs, walls and floors throughout the UK.

Unlike mineral wool, PIR insulation provides a high level of thermal resistance within a relatively small thickness. This makes it particularly useful in Brentwood properties where loft space is limited or where homeowners want to maintain usable storage space.

Many modern loft insulation projects use PIR boards between rafters, above rafters or in specific areas where achieving modern insulation standards would otherwise require a much thicker build-up of traditional insulation materials.

Typical PIR Insulation Board Thicknesses

Rigid PIR insulation boards are commonly available in a range of thicknesses including:

  • 25mm PIR insulation boards
  • 50mm PIR insulation boards
  • 75mm PIR insulation boards
  • 100mm PIR insulation boards
  • 120mm PIR insulation boards
  • 150mm PIR insulation boards

The thickness required depends entirely on the application. There is no single board thickness that suits every loft.

In many Brentwood loft projects we find that 100mm to 150mm PIR boards are commonly used where significant thermal improvements are required, while 50mm or 75mm boards may be installed where space restrictions prevent thicker insulation.

How Thick Should Rigid PIR Insulation Boards Be in a Loft?

For many loft insulation applications, 100mm PIR insulation is often considered a practical starting point. This provides substantial thermal performance while remaining manageable within most roof structures.

However, when insulating pitched roof slopes or creating a warm roof construction, thicker boards may be necessary to achieve the required thermal performance.

Older Brentwood homes often have roof structures that were never designed with modern insulation standards in mind. Because of this, the ideal board thickness frequently depends on the depth of the rafters available.

In some situations, installers may combine insulation layers to achieve better results. For example, insulation may be installed between rafters with an additional insulated layer beneath to reduce thermal bridging.

This is why professional assessment is important before purchasing materials. The correct thickness isn't determined solely by a target number but by the overall roof construction.

Why PIR Boards Can Be Thinner Than Mineral Wool

Homeowners are often surprised when they see a PIR board that is significantly thinner than traditional loft insulation rolls.

The reason is that PIR insulation has a much lower thermal conductivity value than many conventional insulation products. In simple terms, it slows heat transfer more effectively for its thickness.

Traditional loft insulation often requires around 270mm of depth to meet modern recommendations. PIR insulation can achieve strong thermal performance with considerably less thickness, which is why it is commonly used where headroom or storage space is limited.

That does not mean PIR automatically replaces mineral wool in every loft. In many cases the best solution depends on the layout of the loft and how the space is used.

Factors That Affect PIR Insulation Thickness Requirements

Every loft is different. The correct PIR insulation thickness is usually influenced by several factors.

  • Existing insulation already present in the loft
  • Roof construction type
  • Available rafter depth
  • Whether the loft is being used for storage
  • Whether the loft is being converted into habitable space
  • Ventilation requirements
  • Desired energy efficiency improvements

For example, a loft conversion in Brentwood will often require a different insulation approach compared to a standard cold loft where insulation is installed at ceiling level.

Good insulation performance is never just about thickness. Proper installation, ventilation and continuity of insulation are equally important.

Building Regulations and Thermal Performance

UK building regulations focus on achieving certain thermal performance standards rather than simply specifying one insulation thickness.

The required thickness can therefore vary depending on the insulation product being used and the construction details of the roof.

Homeowners wanting to understand current UK guidance can review information available through the government's building regulations guidance here: UK Building Regulations Approved Documents.

When planning any major insulation upgrade, particularly loft conversions or roof alterations, it's worth ensuring the proposed insulation system aligns with current requirements.

Final Thoughts

So, how thick should rigid PIR insulation boards be?

In most loft applications, PIR boards typically range between 50mm and 150mm, with 100mm often being a common starting point. However, the correct thickness depends on the roof design, available space and thermal performance requirements of the property.

The best results come from assessing the loft as a complete system rather than focusing on insulation thickness alone. Ventilation, installation quality and overall coverage all play a major role in long-term performance.
